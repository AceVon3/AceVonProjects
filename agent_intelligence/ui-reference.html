Wage & HourWA
Washington Minimum Wage & Overtime
Washington's minimum wage is $17.13/hour in 2026 and rises each year with inflation. Most employees earn 1.5× pay after 40 hours in a week, regardless of employer size. Tips belong to employees and can't count toward minimum wage.
Sources
lni.wa.gov
lni.wa.gov/overtime

Leave LawsWA
Washington Paid Sick Leave & PFML
Employees earn at least 1 hour of paid sick leave for every 40 hours worked. The state's Paid Family & Medical Leave program offers up to 12 weeks for qualifying events, funded by premiums shared between employer and employee.
Sources
lni.wa.gov
paidleave.wa.gov
Last checked: 2026-05-28
PayrollWA
Washington Employer Payroll Taxes
Employers file quarterly unemployment, Paid Leave, and WA Cares reports through ESD. New employers must first get a business license from the Department of Revenue before filing.
Sources
esd.wa.gov
dor.wa.gov
Last checked: 2026-05-28
Workers' CompWA
Washington Workers' Compensation
Washington is a monopolistic state fund — coverage must be bought through L&I, not a private insurer. Nearly all employers must carry it and report worker hours quarterly.
Sources
lni.wa.gov/insurance
Last checked: 2026-05-28
TerminationWA
Washington Final Pay & Layoff Notice
Final wages are due by the next regular payday whether an employee quits or is fired. Employers with 50+ staff must give 60 days' notice before covered mass layoffs or closures.
Sources
lni.wa.gov
esd.wa.gov
Last checked: 2026-05-28
Nexus & LicensingWA
Washington Producer Licensing
Insurance producers must be licensed through the Office of the Insurance Commissioner, with lines of authority for the products they sell. Businesses also register with the Department of Revenue for tax nexus.
Sources

Washington Hiring Requirements
Employers must meet wage, paid-sick-leave, and workplace-standards obligations from an employee's first day. L&I's employment standards cover the core rules new employers need.
